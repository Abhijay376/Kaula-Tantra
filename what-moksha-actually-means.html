What Moksha Actually Means

It is not one thing. Every tradition describes it a little differently.

People use the word moksha like it means one clear thing. It does not. Different schools of Indian philosophy describe it in quite different ways, and the differences actually matter for how you live.

Some traditions treat moksha as pure stillness. The self becomes aware and nothing else. No pleasure, no activity, just existence. Other traditions go a step further and say the self is also knowing, aware of itself, but still not joyful and still not active.

Vedanta adds joy to the picture. The true self is existence, knowledge, and bliss together. But even there, the liberated self is usually described as still, not doing anything.

Kashmiri Tantra, and Sri Vidya along with it, takes the boldest position. It says the true self is not only existence, knowledge, and bliss, it is also activity. Freedom itself is part of what you are. So a liberated person is not sitting still in some frozen peace. They are alive, active, and joyfully so.

That is closer to what draws me to this path. Moksha here is not about disappearing or shutting down. It is remembering something you forgot you already had, and then living from it, out loud, in your actual life.

A simple way to picture it

Think of cleaning a piece of cloth. You are not adding cleanliness to it. The cloth was always clean underneath. You are only removing what was covering it. Moksha works the same way. Nothing new gets added to you. What was already there just stops being hidden.

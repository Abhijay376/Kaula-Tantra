Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath: Amrit Katha, Part 1

Stories and sayings of the Bengali saint of the divine Name, kept on Guruji's blog.

A collection of stories and sayings of Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath, drawn from books and memoirs by his disciples, including Sri Madhav Swamiji, Kinkar Vitthal Ramanuja, Kinkar Dheerananda and Sri Aditya Kr De Chowdhury. Not written by Guruji. Shared on Guruji's blog, originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath was a Bengali saint known as the apostle of Naam, the divine Name.
  • His five ways to purity: sattvic food, worship on time, celibacy, holy study with chanting, and holy company.
  • He taught that Hari and Hara are one, that every path leads to Omkar, and that a devotee can reach the heights of yoga by chanting alone.
  • Most of the post is stories from his disciples about letters, healings, visions and dacoits who took up chanting.
  • It closes with his rules on guru and mantra, including staying three years with your present guru's practice.

A summary of the collection

Sitaramdas Amrit Katha is a long collection of stories and sayings about Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath, gathered from books and memoirs by his disciples. Very little of it is Guruji's own writing, so this page gives a summary and one short quote. Please read the full collection on Guruji's blog.

It opens with an extract from Sri Sri Omkar Sahasra Lipi listing Thakur's five ways: sattvic food, timely worship, celibacy, holy study with chanting of the Name, and holy company. Holding to these, it says, makes even the lowest person as pure as tulsi at the Lord's feet. Sri Madhav Swamiji, Thakur's close companion, follows with the view that becoming a true sevak is the hardest task, because service burns away vanity that other paths leave behind. He tells devotees to pray "Give me strength" before every task and to offer the result at the end. A couplet from Thakur's Sudhar Dhara makes the same point.

Many stories show his care for devotees. He answered letters from all over India and is said to have known their contents before the envelopes were opened. He told an old woman frying puffed rice to keep chanting because he loved to hear her. He sent a disgraced brahmacharini out of the ashram and, when she asked where she could go, pointed to his own feet. He fed stray dogs at Bithur, honoured the ashram cow (from Sri Sri Lila Chinta), and warned that maligning others leads to hell.

On doctrine, the post says he taught that Hari and Hara are one, quoting the Atharva Shiva Upanishad in his Shiv Namamrita Lahari. Each Purana praises one deity to build devotion to that form and not to put others down. A later section calls his teaching Omkarvaad: whatever path a seeker follows, they reach Omkar Brahma in the end. His verse says that if you keep chanting Ram Naam, no other yoga is needed. When the yogi Dayal Maharaj, a disciple of Sri Shyamacharan Lahiri Mahasay, tried to teach him mudras, he told Thakur he had already finished everything through japa.

There are accounts of healing and protection. The writer Navaneeta Devsen describes how Thakur brought her father, the poet Naren Dev, back from near death. The account is chronicled by Kinkar Vitthal Ramanuja in Nabo Nabo Rupe Eso, in English by Raj Supe (Kinkar Vishwashreyananda). A nurse's statement is given on the recovery of Sri Padmalochan Mukherjee at Calcutta Medical College. Sri Taritbhusan De tells of a vision of Thakur that warned him before he escaped arrest in East Pakistan. Other stories involve Ma Anandamoyee, a snake resting on his head in samadhi, an army doctor, and a band of about 250 dacoits who joined his chanting at Chatak Parbat.

The post also tells of his childhood samadhi at one year old during Harinam, and of Lord Jagannath appearing to him at Puri on 24 April 1937 with the words "Go and spread the Holy Name" (from Purusottam Omkarnath by Sri Madhav Swamiji). It lists his principles on guru and mantra: guru, mantra and Ishta are one, and a devotee should hold to the present guru's mantra for at least three years before seeking guidance elsewhere, with permission where possible. It quotes the ten marks of dharma from the Manusmriti, which he often recited.

"for the sake of removing 'I' [Ego], all worship prayer yoga sacrifice penances are there."

Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath, as quoted in "Sitaramdas Amrit Katha - 1" on Guruji's blog. The page shows no date.

Sources named in the post

The collection draws on these books and people, all credited here as in the original: Sri Sri Omkar Sahasra Lipi; Thakur's Sudhar Dhara, Shiv Namamrita Lahari, Matrigatha, Abhay Vaani and his composition quoted from Maahrasayan; Sri Sri Lila Chinta; Thakur Katha and Purusottam Omkarnath by Sri Madhav Swamiji; Nabo Nabo Rupe Eso by Kinkar Vitthal Ramanuja, in English by Raj Supe (Kinkar Vishwashreyananda), with the account of Navaneeta Devsen; Srichoroner Chhayay Chhayay by Sri Aditya Kr De Chowdhury; Sri Sri Nam Prachar Lila by Kinkar Dheerananda; writings of Kinkar Bhumananda; a poem by Kinkar Ashimananda (Sushil Kr Bandopadhay); a narration by Kinkar Sharanandaji; the memoirs of Sri Shankarnath Chattopadhyay; and accounts by Rev Gopal Pandit Mahasay, Prof Tarasankar Bhattacharya, Prof Amalendu Bhusan Bhattacharya, Sri Puranjan Roy Bandopadhyay and Sri Taritbhusan De. Scripture quoted includes the Atharva Shiva Upanishad and the Manusmriti.

What this means in practice

Thakur's whole path rests on the Name. Kaula practice uses mantra in the same way, as something you keep going under your breath through the day, in the kitchen as much as at the altar. The story of the woman frying puffed rice makes the point well. Her chanting while she worked counted.

His teaching that Hari and Hara are one, and that every Purana praises its own deity only to build devotion, fits a Shakta student well. You can keep one form of Devi as your Ishta and still bow to every other form without rivalry.

His view of service is worth taking into puja. Madhav Swamiji's advice is to ask for strength before you begin and to hand over the result when you finish. That turns outer worship, homam or yantra puja into practice in letting go of "I".

Thakur belongs to a different line from Devipuram. Guruji kept this collection on his blog, which shows how widely he read and honoured other saints. See also Guru Paduka Mantra: Guruji on the Supreme Key for Guruji's own words on the guru.

Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015. Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

Who was Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath?

A Bengali saint known as the apostle of Naam, the divine Name. The post says that on 24 April 1937 at Puri, Lord Jagannath appeared to him and told him to go and give the Naam, and he spent his life spreading it.

What did Sitaramdas Omkarnath teach?

Chanting the Name above all. The post quotes his verse that if you keep chanting Ram Naam, no other yoga is needed. He also taught service to the guru, surrender of the results of work, and respect for every path.

What are Thakur's five ways to purity?

From Sri Sri Omkar Sahasra Lipi: pure (sattvic) food, worship at the right time, celibacy, study of holy books with chanting of the Name, and holy company.

Did Sitaramdas Omkarnath say Vishnu and Shiva are one?

Yes. The post says he taught that Hari and Hara are one, and that each Purana praises one deity to build devotion to that form, never to insult the others.

What did Thakur say about leaving a guru?

According to the principles listed in the post, a devotee should keep to the present guru's mantra and instructions for at least three years. If sincere effort brings no bliss, they may seek further guidance from a great soul, with the guru's permission where possible.

Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath: Amrit Katha, Part 2

More stories and sayings of the Bengali saint of the divine Name, kept on Guruji's blog.

A second collection of stories and sayings of Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath, gathered from memoirs and books by his disciples, including Kinkar Vithhal Ramanuja, Kinkar Atmananda, Kinkar Dhirananda and Gen. Sujan Singh Uban. Not written by Guruji. Shared on Guruji's blog, originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com. Read Part 1 first.

In short

  • These Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath stories pick up where Part 1 ended, with about a hundred short accounts from his disciples.
  • Many show him as a guru who remembered each disciple's mantra, need and stage, and who reached people far from where he sat.
  • He called the Name a medicine for worldly and spiritual illness and said anyone may chant it, whatever their caste or past.
  • He set Gour Nitai beside Ma Kali at Kalna and said that disrespecting any deity disrespects your own Ishta.
  • He warned again and again about harsh speech and criticism, and asked for love as his dakshina.

A summary of the collection

Part 2 of Sitaramdas Amrit Katha is another long run of stories and sayings about Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath, told by the people around him. None of it is Guruji's own writing, so this page gives a summary and one short quote. The full collection is on Guruji's blog.

It opens with glimpses of his power as a guru. A woman who already had all her mantras sat in the diksha line only for his touch, and he smiled and said every one of her old mantras into her ear. A seven foot tall foreigner at Santiniketan asked to know the Soul, fainted when Thakur embraced him, and said afterwards that his question had been answered. Nandarani Ma of Jabalpur, who loved him as her child Gopal, offered him salt by mistake for sugar at a midnight train stop, and he ate all of it. A dacoit chief at Baltikuri handed over his weapons, took a kartal instead, and later took diksha.

Short teachings sit between the stories. Asked whether service to the guru or japa and meditation is higher, he said it depends on your nature: pick one as your main path without dropping the other. Japa of a Name such as Ram, Ma or Guru slows the breath, and then inner sound and light appear. He said devotees think of him because he thinks of them first, like a river that comes from the ocean. To an attendant worried about the future of the ashrams he said that everything has creation, sustenance and destruction, so do your best and leave the rest to the guru (from Nabo Nabo Rupe Eso by Kinkar Vithhal Ramanuja).

Several accounts come from soldiers. Gen. Sujan Singh Uban, whose memoirs appear in The Gurus of India, tells of Thakur's visit to Chakrata, of soldiers near the ashram giving up drink for namkirtan, and of Thakur praying to the Goddess at the Durga temple in Varanasi before the Indo-Pak war: "Ma, Sujan is going to war, protect him." A jawan later recognised Thakur as the saint who caught him when he fell during the Indo-China war. Others tell of the founding of Mahamilan Math in Kolkata in 1965, of a costly watch Thakur threw into the Ganga, and of fruit and fragrance appearing in a locked prayer room on 9 November 1968.

Many stories are about healing and protection: a scorpion bite cured at RG Kar hospital, two devotees revived from electric shock with Padukamrita, a girl with polio helped by tulsi, a gas fire that left a devotee unhurt. The Name is the medicine in most of them. For a woman with mental illness he advised unbroken chanting of the Name.

The collection also covers his lineage. His sampraday traces to Ramananda and is tied to Ramanuja, and eight of his disciples took sannyas at Srirangam during a tour with 61 companions (told by Kinkar Atmananda). There is Dasarathidev's account of how Thakur heard the Hare Krishna Mahamantra as continuous inner sound in a cave at Dumurdaha. He founded the magazine Arya Nari, encouraged women's Sati Sanghas, married a Dutch couple in the church at Puri in December 1978 and named them Yogananda and Yogmaya, and gave initiation in the Harijan quarter of Jalpaiguri, saying the Lord is pleased by love and asking for love as his dakshina.

Through all of it runs his teaching on speech. Hurtful words come back to the speaker, a person who criticises takes on the sins of the one criticised, and scripture used as a weapon against others is foolish. His Bengali verses, translated in the post, ask the devotee to see himself as the Lord's instrument and to stay the same in praise and blame. The last story is of a sweeper woman at Memary who refused her wages and asked for diksha instead.

"Since one’s Ista [aspired Deity] is existing in all Forms, nurturing disrespect for other deities tantamount to disrespecting one’s Ista—this should be kept in mind."

Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath, as quoted in "Sitaramdas Amrit Katha - 2" on Guruji's blog. The page shows no date.

What this means in practice

Thakur's line about the Ishta fits a Shakta student well. You can keep one form of Devi at the centre of your practice and still bow to Ganapati, Shiva, Krishna or any other form, because your Ishta is in all of them. His setting of Gour Nitai beside Ma Kali makes the same point in a temple.

His use of the Name is close to how mantra works in Kaula practice. You keep it going while you cook, travel or work, and the stories treat that quiet chanting as the main practice. Outer worship such as yantra puja or homam sits on top of it.

His warnings about speech are a practice of their own. Watching what you say about other people, and staying steady when you are praised or blamed, is part of treating everyone as a form of the Goddess.

Thakur belongs to a different line from Devipuram. Guruji kept this collection on his blog. Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015, and Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

Who was Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath?

A Bengali saint known for spreading Naam, the divine Name. The post describes his sampraday as tracing back to Ramananda and tied to Ramanuja, with ashrams and maths across India.

What did Sitaramdas Omkarnath say about the Name?

He called it a medicine for both worldly and spiritual illness. He said anyone may chant it, whatever their caste or past, and that japa of the Name slows the breath until inner sound and light appear.

Is guru seva or japa better, according to Thakur?

He said neither is higher. It depends on your nature, so choose one as your main path without dropping the other.

Did Thakur Sitaramdas Omkarnath honour other deities?

Yes. He set Gour Nitai beside Ma Kali at Kalna and taught that because your Ishta is present in all forms, disrespecting another deity disrespects your own.

Where is the Mahamantra story in this collection?

The post gives Dasarathidev's account that Thakur heard the Hare Krishna Mahamantra as continuous inner sound in a cave at Dumurdaha.

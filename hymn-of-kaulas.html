Hymn of the Kaulas Explained

A Sanskrit hymn of blessing and protection for the Kaula path, shared by Guruji in Sir John Woodroffe's translation.

Shared by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji) on his page at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com. The hymn itself is from the seventh chapter of the Kaulavalinirnaya, translated into English by Sir John Woodroffe.

In short

  • This hymn comes from the seventh chapter of the Kaulavalinirnaya, an old Tantric text.
  • Guruji shared the full hymn on his page, in Sir John Woodroffe's English translation.
  • It opens with a bow to the guardian deity of the Kaula path, Lord Vatuka.
  • Most verses are blessings: for practitioners to succeed, and for teachers and the tradition to be protected.
  • A few verses ask for protection from people who mock or oppose the tradition, in the strong language typical of old devotional hymns.
  • This is not Guruji's own writing. It is a classical hymn he valued enough to share in full, with credit to its translator.

A line from the hymn

"I bow to the Sovereign Lord of the followers of Kula. He is the all-spreading Chintamani the impulse in the heart of all Sadhakas. What wonderful fruits based in Dharma does his worship done in manner enjoined (Vidhina) yield!"

Verse 1, Hymn of the Kaulas, Kaulavalinirnaya chapter 7, translated by Sir John Woodroffe. Shared in full by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji) at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

How this shows up in Kaula practice

Hymns like this one are usually not the main event of a practice. They are how a session opens: a way of turning attention toward a guardian or deity before the deeper worship begins, whether that worship happens inwardly, through the five elements of nature, or through an outer yantra, homam or deity puja.

The strong language in the later verses, asking for the tradition's detractors to be undone, is common in old devotional hymns of this kind. It reads as a wish for the path itself to be safe and to continue, not as a claim this site makes about anyone alive today.

Common questions

What is the Hymn of the Kaulas?

It is a devotional hymn from the seventh chapter of the Kaulavalinirnaya, a classical Tantric text. Guruji shared it on his blog page in the English translation made by Sir John Woodroffe.

Who is Lord Vatuka, according to the hymn?

The hymn describes Vatuka as a fierce guardian form, dressed in red, with flaming tawny matted hair and a crescent moon on his forehead. He is invoked to remove obstacles for practitioners.

What does the hymn ask for?

Most of it is a string of blessings: success and completion for practitioners (Sadhakas), well-being for teachers and students of the Kula path, and protection of the tradition from those who mock or attack it.

What language is the hymn originally written in?

Sanskrit. Guruji's page gives each verse in transliterated Sanskrit followed by Sir John Woodroffe's English translation.

Role of the Guru: Guruji on Spirituality as Divine Romance

Guruji on what a real Guru does, why the world mirrors you, and why he calls spiritual life a Divine romance.

Written by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji). Post title: "Spirituality is not dull and boring thing, it is Divine romance", posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 5 November 2016. In it Guruji refers to Ramana Maharshi, Sri Aurobindo's Savitri, Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, Mahatma Gandhi, the Buddha's last message to Ananda and the film The Matrix. The original also has a photo of Guruji, which is not copied here. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • The role of the Guru, Guruji says, is to kill your ego and let you be reborn as the world.
  • The Guru connects you to the power of the universe, Ādi Parā Śakti, and tells you that you are already an awakened being.
  • He names Ramana Maharshi, Aurobindo and Ramakrishna Paramahamsa as saints who went through this death of the ego.
  • The world reflects you. Hate it and it hates you back; change yourself and it changes. He uses the bending spoon from The Matrix to show this.
  • Gandhi counted his own ego among his three enemies, and the Buddha's last words to Ananda were "Be a light unto yourself."
  • Vedānta says the world is śūnya. Tantra says the world has power, and you can manifest it as long as you control Māyā.

Guruji's words

Guru helps to connect to the power of the universe, i.e., Ādi Parā Śakti.
The function of any real Guru is to kill your ego first — die first — and then be reborn as the world. That is the algorithm. The Guru does that.

The connection to the circle and the dot is an empowering act, to becoming a winner.

In the history of every saint, they have gone through a transformation from their physical nature to astral nature.
If you take Ramana Maharshi, he enacted his death and was born spiritually.
Similarly Aurobindo wrote Savitri — the whole book is about the ascent and descent of Satyavan.
With Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, he did everything to kill his ego.

So the job of the Guru is to kill you — i.e., your concept of being only a physical entity.
You have body, mind, but you are not them.

The world is contained in you.

Job of the Guru is to tell you that you are an awakened being.
All you have to do is overcome limitations — that which binds you.

Spirituality is not a dull and boring thing.
It is Divine romance.
Loving all and being loved by all.

All the gopis (Krishna līlā) are modifications in our life.
Why do we love the world?
Because the world out there is your own impression.

The Guru tells you this.
You start experiencing that.

When you are improving, the world improves.
If you hate the world, the world hates you — it has no option.
It reflects you, since it is yourself.

If you move, the world moves.

If you have watched the movie Matrix, where a boy looks at a spoon — if he moves his head, the spoon also bends.
How does he manage?
The boy says there is no spoon there.
I am bending my mind, and the reflection of mind — i.e., the spoon — bends.

You have power to control destiny and make it as you want.
You can bend the world.

None of us makes the future alone.
All contribute to the future.

When you change, the world changes.

Leadership implies first committing to change in the way you want to.

When you understand that your ego is just a small, small part of the whole.

For Mahatma Gandhi, he had three enemies:

  1. British rule of India
  2. Attitude of Indians in depending blindly on karma theory
  3. Considered himself as an enemy — i.e., one’s own ego

Buddha told Ananda his last message:
“Don’t be guided by anything other than you. Be self-referential. Be proactive. Be a light unto yourself.”
This was when Ananda asked in tears for a last message.

Essence of Śrīvidyā is in the four Mahāvākyas — principles of Vedānta.

Vedānta says:
Yes, you are the world. The world is śūnya.

Tantra says:
You are the world. The world has power.
As long as you control Māyā, you can manifest power.

Bringing the conceptualization of universal principles into human realms is possible.

"Spirituality is not dull and boring thing, it is Divine romance" by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati, as posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 5 November 2016.

What this means in practice

Kaula practice starts from the idea Guruji gives here: the world is contained in you. In inner worship you treat your own body and mind as the place where the Devi lives, and the outer world becomes a reflection you can work with.

His line about the circle and the dot points to the Sri Chakra, whose centre is the bindu. Sitting before a yantra, or holding the bindu in mind during meditation, is one way people practise that connection.

The mirror teaching is very practical. If the world reflects you, start with your own anger, fear or dislike. Guruji says that when you improve, the world improves.

He calls all this Divine romance: loving all and being loved by all. In Kaula practice that love takes in nature and the five elements, which are honoured as forms of the Goddess.

Common questions

What is the role of a Guru?

Guruji says a real Guru kills your ego, meaning your idea that you are only a physical body, so you can be reborn as the world. The Guru also tells you that you are already awakened and only need to overcome what binds you.

What does "spirituality is Divine romance" mean?

Guruji says spirituality means loving all and being loved by all. He links it to Krishna's play with the gopis and says we love the world because it is our own impression.

Does the world reflect your inner state?

In this talk Guruji says it does. If you hate the world, it hates you back because it reflects you. When you change, the world changes.

What was the Buddha's last message to Ananda?

Guruji quotes it as "Don't be guided by anything other than you. Be self-referential. Be proactive. Be a light unto yourself." Ananda had asked for it in tears.

How do Vedānta and Tantra see the world differently?

Both say you are the world. Vedānta adds that the world is śūnya, empty. Tantra says the world has power, and you can manifest it as long as you control Māyā.

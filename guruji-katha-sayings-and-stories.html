Guruji Katha: Sayings and Stories of Devipuram Guruji

Short sayings, answers and memories of Guruji, collected by his disciple Vikram.

Sayings of Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji), collected by his disciple Vikram. Posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 6 November 2016. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Guruji Katha is a long collection of Devipuram Guruji's sayings, put together by his disciple Vikram, often with a note on when and to whom each was said.
  • Guruji puts the Guru Paduka mantra above all others. Other mantras come and go, he says, but this one stays with you.
  • He explains the Siva Linga at four levels, from physical union up to a state where the seer and the seen are one.
  • To be free of the fear of death, he says you have to die three times: to the idea that you are the body, to the idea that you are the mind, and to the belief that death ends life.
  • Many lines are about ordinary life. Decide to be happy whatever others say, give before you take, and treat profit and loss the same.
  • Disciples add their own memories, down to his last words on the phone a day before his mahasamadhi.

Me, world, Śiva, Śakti are all illusions. This is the greatest vidyā. I don’t exist. So I cannot be created, nor destroyed. Time, space, matter, knowledge, action don’t exist. This has been called Mokṣa.

Because you are the world, you see the world. You also know that you are the only person to consult when a decision is to be made. That makes deciding an extremely simple process.

The lowest level at which we understand the Śiva Liṅga is at the level of physical union. It is the male phallus which is called Śiva and the female yoni which is called Pārvatī. It is their union that we are worshiping. There, the union is for procreation.

At the next highest level, the fire in the navel center and the love in the heart center, their union is called Śiva and Śakti.

In the next higher level still, space is considered the womb and time is the liṅga which is moving. The universe of space and time is called the Śiva Liṅga.

All the five elements, all the ways in which we perceive, all these are considered to be liṅgas.

After these three levels, we reach the fourth and transcendent level where there is no distinction between the seer and the seen. They have become one. That is the highest form of the liṅga. It is called aliṅga. There is no liṅga there. There is no characteristic that differentiates one from the other. You are joined so totally with the world. You have absorbed the whole world into yourself and there is nothing other than you. So, to reach that state is called mokṣa.

What is consciousness? It is the mirror reflecting you to yourself.

She does not connect the arrow to the bow, which means that Devī represents the yogic state where you decouple your mind from disturbing sensory inputs. But even when this mind is decoupled from the senses, there is still another part of the mind which is the memory. The memory contains in it all the senses and it keeps on bringing these things up. All those things also have to be decoupled. The mind itself has to go. It is then that you are flowing in the cosmic awareness.

What this means in practice

Most of these sayings need no ritual. "Make your whole life a prayer" and "Intention is all that matters" can go with you into an ordinary working day. When a busy disciple said he had no time for sadhana, Guruji told him to do as much as he could, because being focused in whatever you do, even a job, is worship.

Inner worship comes up again and again. He says the real Devipuram is your body, and that giving your full attention to each part of the body widens awareness until you can feel the Mother and become one with Her. This is where body worship in our lineage starts. For outer worship he keeps it simple too: talk to the Sri Chakra and ask Her to protect you when you leave the house, and thank Her when you come back.

Some sayings touch on the Kaula side of the path, where sex, the body and taboo are brought into worship. We keep his words as they were written. Our own work with these ideas is explained philosophically and done privately. Where he speaks of seeing the Goddess in a girl, a single woman or a couple, our practice involves only willing, consenting adults, and a Kumari is always above 18. He also gives the rule himself: the woman being worshipped decides where the line is, and if it is crossed, it is no longer worship.

Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015. Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

What is Guruji Katha?

It is a collection of sayings and short stories of Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati of Devipuram, gathered by his disciple Vikram. It was posted on Guruji's blog in November 2016, and many entries say who he was speaking to and where.

Why did Guruji call the Guru Paduka mantra the most important?

He said paduka means the Guru's sandals, and if you wear them, they know where to take you. Other mantras can come and go, but this one stays with you and takes you from the root chakra up to the brain.

What are the four levels of the Siva Linga in Guruji's teaching?

The first is the physical union of male and female. The next is the fire at the navel joined with the love in the heart, and then space as the womb with time as the moving linga. The last is alinga, where there is no difference between the seer and the seen.

What does Guruji mean by dying three times?

He said freedom from the fear of death has a price. You let go of the idea that you are the body, then the idea that you are the mind, and then the belief that death is the end of life.

What were Guruji's last words to his disciples?

According to the collection, he said over the phone, a day before he left his body: "I am going. Wish you all the best."

Suppressed Desires: Guruji on Desire and the Nude Goddesses

Guruji writes on what happens when we push our desires down, and what the nude goddesses of Devipuram say to us in their own voice.

Written by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji), signed "Amrita". Post title: "Nudity and Desires: Suppression or expression?", posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 5 November 2016. The original has a photo of Guruji, which is not copied here. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Suppressed desires build tension, Guruji says. Over time that tension grows into impatience, anxiety, anger, hatred and in the end violence.
  • He offers another way: look at desire with respect, as a lovely expression of life, and turn lust into affection.
  • The nude goddesses speak in his poem. They have nothing to hide, they find nothing impure in God's creation, and their love has no conditions.
  • He says this is Indian heritage, since the culture honours Kama, desire, as the third aim of life. What gets imported is exploitation.
  • He explains the Siva Linga as the union of universal male and female, and Gayatri as the life giving power of the Sun.
  • Different groups worship the Devi in different ways, and he ends by asking each reader to choose a path that fits their own nature.

Guruji's words

Any kind of desire suppressed creates tension.
The more we suppress, the more the tension.
Over time, tension builds up to lack of patience,
anxiety, anger, hatred, and finally destructive violence.
This temple shows your subconscious desires to you openly.
Which you do not want to admit because society looks down on it.
You want to be known as not interested in desires.
So you suppress your deepest desires. For sex. For affection. For kindness.
Vested interests make money out of your suppressed desires by flesh trade.
There is another way. By looking on desire with respect and adoration.
As life's lovely expression. In calm contemplation of your own nature.
By converting lust into affection.
The nude goddesses here teach you precisely that.
Are they worried whether you laugh at them or look at their private parts?
They are saying, "look this is how I am. I have nothing to hide.
In God's creation, there is nothing impure, even the genital.
I am in tune with my nature. I love myself for being just what I am.
I love you. I love all living beings.

You don't have to love me. I still love you. My love has no conditions."
"I am the fresh lotus that opens up to the light of love.
You are the light. Even if you don't believe it now.
I tell you, your nature is divine. You are God.
And I am your Nature. Lovely, full breasted, caring, self offering.
I am yours. I am your nature. You think I am not yours. So you desire me.
I am unveiling myself, so that you can see your own nature.
I am the gate from which you came.
I am the gate through which you will realise that you are God."
"I want to teach you how to be kind, affectionate. If you allow me.
I share every thing I have with you. My most precious thing, even modesty.
I offer myself to you completely. All my powers to do good.
Power to think good thoughts. Power to speak them out. Power to do them.
I cant teach you how to be greedy, aggressive, angry or violent.
They do not belong to divine nature. They are human emotions.
There is no trading in love."
Do not think that these ideas are not our culture, that it is imported.
Our culture worships Kama, desire, as the third Purushartha.
This is not imported culture. What we import is exploitation, slavery.
We import titillating excitement and sensuality, not adoration.
Our own culture is supposed to worship and respect woman.
Especially if she is from low class. Like a washer woman or a tribal girl.
This is our tantric and vedic heritage.
It is our tradition to worship Siva Linga.
Siva Linga is the union of universal male with universal female.
We respect sex to take away its bad influences.
It is our tradition to worship Gayatri or any Devi.
Gayatri is the life giving power coming from the Sun.
She wakes you up every morning from sleep with Her life force.
Gayatri is a Vedic Goddess, accessible only to a few Brahmins.
However, Her tantric Devis here are accessible to all castes equally.
Brahmins worship Her with milk. Kshatriyas with ghee. Vysyas with honey.
Others with liquor. Brahmins offer Her vegetarian dishes and abstain from sex.
All other castes offer Her meat and engage in ritual sex on specific days.
Brahmins worship Her in a fire pit or in a Sri Chakra.
Sri Chakra is really a circle of devotees who are doing sadhana together.
Four males and five females together constitute one Sri Chakra.
Kaulas worship Devi in the genitals of females.
Choose your path according to your own inclinations.
We are with you.

What this means in practice

The first half of this teaching is something you can work with in meditation. When a desire comes up, Guruji asks you to look at it calmly, with respect, as part of your own nature. Hiding it from yourself is what builds the tension he describes.

In Kaula self worship, the goddesses' words make a good contemplation: "I am unveiling myself, so that you can see your own nature." Sit with a yantra or an image of the Devi and read the poem slowly, letting it speak to you.

Guruji lists many forms of outer worship, from milk offerings to the fire pit to the Sri Chakra as a circle of people doing sadhana together. On this site we talk about these only at the level of meaning. Any Kaula practice that honours a living person as the Goddess takes place only between willing, consenting adults.

He closes with "Choose your path according to your own inclinations. We are with you." No one path is forced on anyone. A teacher can help you see which one fits you.

Common questions

What happens when you suppress your desires?

Guruji says suppressed desire creates tension. The more we suppress, the more tension we carry, and over time it builds into impatience, anxiety, anger, hatred and finally violence.

How do you deal with desire in spirituality?

He suggests looking on desire with respect and adoration, as an expression of life, and calmly reflecting on your own nature. In this way lust can turn into affection.

What do the nude goddesses teach?

In Guruji's poem the goddesses say they have nothing to hide and that nothing in God's creation is impure. They love all beings without conditions and unveil themselves so you can see your own nature.

Is Kama part of Indian culture?

Yes, Guruji says. Indian culture honours Kama, desire, as the third Purushartha, or aim of life. He says what is imported is exploitation and cheap excitement, not respect for desire.

What is the Siva Linga according to Guruji?

He describes the Siva Linga as the union of the universal male with the universal female. He says we respect sex in this way to take away its bad influences.

How the Goddess Tests You Through Hard Times

A disciple's own account of loss and crisis, and Guruji's words on why the testing never really stops until you stand firm.

Written by Vira Chandra, a disciple of Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji), with Guruji's own words quoted within. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Vira Chandra says his real spiritual growth came through hard years, not through calm ones.
  • He names what he went through: depression, war, betrayal, cancer treatment, and loss.
  • He says none of this broke him. It burned away what was not real and left something clear.
  • He quotes Guruji: the Goddess tests a person right up to the last minute, to see if they will stand firm.
  • He says the blog itself was never really his. He calls himself a wire that grace passed through.
  • He ends by asking that even the sense of "being the one who was tested" be let go too.

Vira Chandra's words, in full

People often associate spiritual clarity with peace, an ashram porch on the quiet slopes of Arunachala, a mind ripened through steady practice. And often, that is true: deep realization germinates in calm, cultivated soil.

Nearly ten years ago, shortly after my Guruji, Amritananda Natha Saraswati, left his body, I began a blog, not as someone seeking grace, but as one already touched by it. The current of the Goddess was alive in me, and I believed my initiation had begun. But as the years unfolded, it became clear: my true initiation came not through serenity, but through relentless adversity.

To taste Amrta-ananda, the nectar of bliss, one must first drink deeply of visa, the poison of profound trials.

Strangely, an unexpected lucidity emerged right from the heart of chaos. Like a superconductor that transmits current with zero resistance once temperatures fall below a critical threshold, consciousness, when stripped by crisis, began to transmit grace with less obstructions. Depression. War. Betrayal. Oncology. Loss. These forces did not break the mind, they clarified it. Where everything familiar was burnt away, something unburnable remained.

This clarity is no personal achievement. It is pure anugraha, the unearned grace of Kali and Bhairava, moving through the form of Guruji, Amriteshwar Bhairava. There is no pride in this, only awe. I was not the cause of this current. I was simply the wire cleared by fire.

I recall Guruji's words:

"She keeps testing me to this day, testing how steadfast I am. My God, the number of tests She puts you through is unimaginable. She'll take you right up to the last minute to test whether you're going to stand on your faith. And that's exactly when you have to stand firm, because only then will She give it to you."

I write, therefore, not to speak of myself, but to bow before this fierce compassion and grace: adversity as the kiln that smelts ego, sorrow as the solvent that dissolves illusion. Let the sacred fire continue its work, until even the idea of refinement disappears, leaving only transparent awareness.

This blog somehow became a stream of that grace. Without promotion, it crossed a million views. How many reposts it gathered across the hidden folds of Facebook and private forums, only the Goddess knows. I was merely nimitta-matram, a transparent instrument in Her hands.

And if even that transparency still carries the faint scent of identity, of one shaped by fire and adversity, of one made into an instrument, may that too be surrendered. Just as the final stick used to stir the pyre must be cast into the flames, let even these senses of egoistical self be offered, quietly, without fanfare, until only Self remains.

How this shows up in Kaula practice

Kaula practice does not treat hard times as a mistake or a punishment. It treats them as part of the ground you work with. Inner worship, sitting with your own body and mind as they actually are, becomes harder to fake when life is falling apart, and that is exactly when it becomes most real.

Guruji's line about being tested "right up to the last minute" points to something practical: do not expect the hardest stretch to be a sign you are doing it wrong. In this account, it was the opposite. The five elements that make up the body and the world do not stop moving through loss, grief or illness. Practice is staying present with that movement instead of turning away from it.

The idea of being a "wire cleared by fire" also fits how Kaula practice sees the self: not something to polish forever, but something to see through. The goal is not becoming a better version of a separate self. It is noticing that the separate self was never quite the point.

Common questions

Why does the Goddess test people, according to Guruji?

Guruji says the testing shows how steady a person's faith really is. He says the tests can carry on right up to the last minute, to see if the person will stand firm before grace is given.

Can hardship actually help spiritual growth?

In this account, yes. The writer says his real turning point came through depression, loss and illness, not through calm and comfort. He says hard times burned away what was not real and left something clear behind.

What does "only the unburnable remains" mean?

It means that once fire, in this case suffering, burns away everything that can be lost, what is left is what was never really yours to lose in the first place: plain awareness itself.

Are You the Creator of Your Own World?

A short, strange conversation between a father and son that stuck with me.

There is a conversation in this tradition, written as an exchange between a spiritual father and his son, that I keep coming back to. The son asks why, if he is really the creator of his own world, the world does not simply obey him.

The father's answer is that the world you create has a life of its own, the same way a child you raise eventually has a life separate from yours. Creating something does not mean controlling it forever.

The conversation moves through the states most of us already know: waking, dreaming, and sleep, and adds two less familiar ones, a waking dream where you have some control over what you are imagining, and a waking sleep where thought and feeling both fall quiet and only awareness itself remains.

The point, as I understand it, is not that you can bend physical reality by wanting something hard enough. It is that your sense of being small, stuck, and separate from your own life is itself a kind of story, and stories can be worked with, layer by layer, the same way this tradition works with the layers of the Sri Chakra.

Why this connects to the poojas

Several of the practices offered here, especially Navavarana Pooja, work through exactly this idea: peeling back one layer of assumed identity at a time, until what is left is something closer to your actual, unguarded self.

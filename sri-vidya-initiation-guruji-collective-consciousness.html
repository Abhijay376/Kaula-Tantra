Sri Vidya Initiation: Guruji on Awakening Collective Power

Guruji explains what initiation passes from Guru to disciple, and why Sri Vidya starts with Ganapati.

Written by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji). Posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 7 November 2016. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Sri Vidya initiation, in Guruji's words, passes on the power to imagine, nourish and bring about a world that supports your vision.
  • The Guru sends that power and the disciple receives it, by waking up to the sleeping powers already there.
  • Sri Vidya dissolves matter into spirit in seven steps: solid, liquid, fire, air, space, time and the unknown.
  • The first step, solid to liquid, is Ganapati. Perfumed water poured over a small pyramid melts the idea of being only this body.
  • When people do this together, their separate awareness merges into one. Guruji works out that 70,000 people acting as one could change the world.

Guruji's words

Transferring power to imagine, nourish, and manifest an environment which supports your vision is initiation. It happens by making you understand your true potential. There is no higher purpose than to know the sleeping powers you have and that you can reinvent your destiny by tuning into the universal mind. The transmitter of such powers is the Guru; the receiver is the disciple.

Dissolve matter into spirit is the theme song of Śrīvidyā. It starts explaining concepts of solidity and shape, the identity = ego we are taught to assume. It proceeds in seven steps:

Solid
Liquid
Fire
Air
Space
Time
Unknown

The first step, moving from solid to liquid, meaning shape to shapelessness, is described as Gaṇapati in the tradition of Śrīvidyā. The ritual makes a paste of impurities connected to the idea of my body. With mantra repetition, spoon after spoon of perfumed water is poured over a pyramid, a symbol of spirit encased in a body. Your actions dissolve the pyramid, and you experience flowing out of your body, an ecstasy. Along with the body, the individual consciousness dissolves into collective consciousness.

Let me explain. Let us suppose that we place four pyramids in a big plate, and four of us do this ritual together. Initially each pyramid (each of us) contains its own awareness. As the pyramids dissolve, the awarenesses leak out and in the end, they all become one.

What have we achieved? Four separate persons have become one single person. The idea of this person contains all the ideas of all of us. So you know the thoughts of the remaining three of us as your own thoughts. We don’t have to tell them to you.

The first step in Śrīvidyā is giving the mantra of Gaṇapati. Why? To enlarge your ideas of who you are. To let the people you know support your ideas, and you support theirs. If n people become one, the power each one has is n(n−1). If ten persons become one, each of them gets ninety times more power than they have now. The power increases exponentially. The happiness increases similarly. Ease of solving problems increases similarly. Problems which loomed large become trivial now.

The key is to tune into collective consciousness, to be able to read the mind of Goddess Nature. The ideas rising in the collective are not exactly the same as the ideas in fragmented minds. So there is no risk of wanting the wrong thing.

If we want to transform a world having 5000 million people, we need only the square root of that number of people. What is it? Square root of 5000 is 70, and that of a million is a thousand. So 70,000 people can decide to change the world and own the power to do it. Together we can change the society. Individually I have very little influence.

What this means in practice

Guruji describes initiation as waking up to powers you already have. The Guru passes them on, and the disciple learns to use them.

The Ganapati step he describes is outer worship with an inner aim. Water, mantra and a small pyramid are the tools, and the goal is to stop feeling locked inside one body. It is also the first of the five elements, earth melting into water. We write about this offering in Ganapati Tarpanam: Guruji's 444 Water Offerings.

He also talks about practising together. When a small group sits and does the same worship, ideas stop belonging to one person. Guruji says the ideas that come from the shared mind are safer than those from a fragmented one. This is the part of the teaching to carry into daily life: support the people around you, and let them support you.

Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015. Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

What is initiation in Sri Vidya?

Guruji defines initiation as passing on the power to imagine, nourish and manifest a world that supports your vision. It works by helping you understand your true potential.

Who gives initiation, and who receives it?

Guruji says the Guru transmits these powers and the disciple receives them. The aim is to know the sleeping powers you already have.

Why does Sri Vidya start with Ganapati?

Guruji says the Ganapati mantra comes first to enlarge your idea of who you are. It is the step from solid to liquid, from shape to shapelessness.

What are the seven steps of Sri Vidya?

Guruji lists them as solid, liquid, fire, air, space, time and the unknown. Together they dissolve matter into spirit.

What is collective consciousness in Sri Vidya?

Guruji describes it as what happens when separate awarenesses merge into one. People in it know each other's thoughts and gain more power, ease and happiness together.

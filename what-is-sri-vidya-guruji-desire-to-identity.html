What Is Sri Vidya? Guruji on Desire, Identity and Advaita

Guruji answers two plain questions: what Sri Vidya is, and why it is worth learning.

Written by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji). Posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 7 November 2016. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • What is Sri Vidya? Guruji calls it understanding how this world came to be, and your role in it.
  • He pictures a collective consciousness like an ocean. We swim in it like fish, and a single drop can hold the whole ocean.
  • He traces the name to Strividya. The letters stand for sattva (knowing), tamas (I and mine), rajas (breaking out) and desire.
  • Take away the t for tamas and Stri becomes Sri, the wealth and pleasure of creating the world.
  • Sri Vidya enjoys what comes without chasing it or pushing it away. It turns the fire of passion into a mother's caring love for all.

Guruji's words

What is Śrīvidyā?

Śrīvidyā is understanding how this world came to be, and my role in it. It proves step by step that there is a collective consciousness like an ocean in which we swim like fishes, breathing in the waters of life. It also helps in understanding how a little drop can contain an ocean of which it is but a small part.

Is it important to learn it?

Śrīvidyā lets you feel the unbelievable wonder of your identity with Brahmā. Brahmā creates all our worlds with the help of Sarasvatī. She is the explosive flow of information. Brahmā is gravity which binds energy to a form. Information which can’t be bound by gravity is Sarasvatī. These are the two fields manifesting life: gravity and waves of presence, called information.

Śrīvidyā is derived from Strīvidyā (notice the extra “t”?), which means adoring nature consisting of the syllables:
s for sattva,
t for tamas,
r for rajas,
and i for desire.

Sattva means knowing, information.
Tamas means the ideas of who I am and who are my family members; in short, I and mine, which are limiting bondages.
Rajas means escaping binding, going out of boundaries of I and mine.
Desire doesn’t choose. It likes binding, gets bored of it and then likes freedom; it gets bored of it too. It keeps oscillating between sympathy and apathy.

If “t” for tamas is removed from Strī, it becomes Śrī, the wealth and pleasure of creating the world.

Śrīvidyā helps to let go of bondage to material things, and to become pure information. Adoring nature without desire is Śrīvidyā. It does not run after objects of desire, neither does it reject them. It does enjoy them when they come, but without bondage.

Śrīvidyā transforms the fiery love of passion into an affectionate love like Mother Nature, which created and cares for all living beings. It gives freedom to love all. It discovers that if you really love some person, that person’s happiness should truly be your own happiness; it can only happen in identity of the lovers.

We can appreciate Śrīvidyā as teaching that “all is one”: non-duality = Advaita in practice.

"Sri Vidya — From Desire to Identity (Advaita in Practice)", by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati, as posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 7 November 2016. The text is given in full. The original post includes two photos of Guruji, which are not copied here.

What this means in practice

Guruji sums up Sri Vidya as adoring nature without being bound by desire. You don't run after things and you don't push them away. When they come, you enjoy them and let them go.

In Kaula practice this shows up first in inner worship. When you sit with the breath and the body as Her home, you watch desire rise, pull you and then tire. Noticing that swing is the start. Outer worship of the five elements and the yantra trains the same eye, so you see nature as Her and adore it.

The last part of his note is about love. Guruji says real love means the other person's happiness becomes your own. That is the meaning of "all is one" in daily life. Our page What Sri Vidya Actually Means looks at the same path from another angle.

Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015. Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

What is Sri Vidya in simple words?

Guruji says Sri Vidya is understanding how this world came to be, and your own role in it. It shows step by step that we live inside one shared consciousness, like fish in an ocean.

What is the meaning of the word Sri Vidya?

Guruji traces it to Strividya, adoring nature. The letters stand for sattva, tamas, rajas and desire. Remove the t for tamas and Stri becomes Sri, the wealth and pleasure of creating the world.

Is Sri Vidya the same as Advaita?

Guruji ends his note by calling Sri Vidya the teaching that all is one. In his words, it is non-duality, Advaita in practice.

Does Sri Vidya ask you to give up desire?

Guruji says Sri Vidya does not run after objects of desire and does not reject them either. It enjoys them when they come, but without bondage.

Why is it important to learn Sri Vidya?

Guruji says it lets you feel your identity with Brahma, who creates our worlds with Sarasvati. It also turns passion into a caring love for all beings.

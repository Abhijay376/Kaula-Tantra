Guruji's Balaji Temple Vision: The 1977 Turning Point

A summary of the night in Hyderabad when a scientist with no religious practice climbed to the Balaji temple, prostrated, and came away changed.

An excerpt from the book The Goddess and the Guru by Michael Bowden, about Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji). Shared on Guruji's blog as "On Turning-Point Hill", posted by Vira Chandra on 5 November 2016. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Guruji's Balaji temple vision happened in 1977, on a work trip to Hyderabad, when he was in his mid-40s and had no religious practice.
  • Unable to sleep, he walked out at 4 a.m. and climbed Naubath Pahad, "Turning-Point Hill", to the Balaji temple at the top.
  • He prostrated without meaning to, felt a thrill through his whole body, and saw Balaji, who named himself as Venkateswara, Bala and Bala Tripurasundari.
  • Guruji took this as his first initiation. Bala became his first guru, and he resolved to use the rest of his life for the good of everyone.
  • He began meditating at night, treating it as observation of his own mind. On the third night he had a frightening vision of exploding into light and stopped for a while.

A summary of the excerpt

This post is an extract from a book by another writer, so it is summarised here and not copied in full. The full extract is on Guruji's blog.

In 1977 Guruji was based in Bombay and travelled for work. A trip to the Electronics Corporation of India in Hyderabad left him restless in his hotel near Nampally Station. At about 4 a.m. he gave up on sleep and walked until he reached the stairs of Naubath Pahad, which the book translates as "Turning-Point Hill". A Balaji temple shone at the top, with a Hanuman temple halfway up. The Hanuman temple reminded him of one he visited as a boy in Vizag.

At that time he kept no religious practice. He describes himself as neutral, with a questioning attitude that came close to irreligious: why believe in a God you cannot see or verify? He later called this "pure arrogance of science." Amma remembers that when their daughter Radha was born in 1963, his mother made a vow to Lord Venkateswara and Guruji objected to bargaining with God. Even years later he would not go into the Tirupati temple.

That morning he felt pushed up the steps. He put vermillion on his forehead at the Hanuman temple and reached the Balaji temple as music played. When an old man ahead of him prostrated, Guruji did the same without thinking. He describes a thrill that lasted ten or fifteen minutes and ran deeper than hair standing on end, as if every atom of his body was thrilling. He saw the universe spin into him, and Balaji spoke: "I am Lord Venkateswara; I am Bala, Balaji and Bala Tripurasundari." The book adds that the last name usually refers to the child form of Lalita, central to Sri Vidya. Balaji then recited the Purnamadah verse, given in Sanskrit and English in the extract.

Less than half an hour had passed. Guruji counted the experience as diksha, a formal initiation, and said Bala became his first spiritual guru. He made a note to look into the spiritual side of life, and decided to use whatever life was left to him for the welfare of everyone and not for destruction. Outwardly little changed. Amma only thought it odd that he had done a full prostration.

He kept seeing Balaji in the years after, usually as the child goddess Bala Tripurasundari. The vision also made him ask why he gave so much to his profession and so little to his inner life. He began to treat meditation as a kind of scientific observation in which the observer and the observed are both himself. He sat late at night while the family slept, listening to an inner humming sound he had known as a boy, which rose in pitch and passed into visible experiences.

The extract includes an image of forming galaxies with Guruji's comment on it: "Can You imagine how much violence exists in this picture of creating galaxies?" On the third night of meditation he woke to what felt like a bomb going off in his heart, with noise and blinding light, his body thrown to the ends of the galaxies. For a moment he saw about ten Sanskrit verses on a screen and caught only "Isavasyam idam sarvam". Frightened, and thinking of his wife and daughters, he decided to stop. He did not tell Amma the details. When he woke afraid, she would tell him to drink some water and go back to sleep.

So it did indeed mark a turning point in my life, from a search for truth in the external direction to a search for truth in an inward direction.

Short quote, Guruji as told in The Goddess and the Guru by Michael Bowden. Shared on Guruji's blog: "On Turning-Point Hill", 5 November 2016, posted by Vira Chandra. The post opens with the note "(from Goddess and the Guru)". It also includes a Sanskrit verse with an English rendering, a photo of Guruji, a photo of Balaji and a galaxy image, which are not reproduced here.

What this means in practice

In Kaula practice Bala is often the first form of the Goddess a student meets, and Guruji's story fits that. His first guru came as a child goddess, and she came to a man who did not believe. You do not need faith in place before you start. The Bala meditation guide is one simple way in.

His meditation was plain watching. He sat, listened to the sound inside, and let what he saw change him as he watched it. That is inner worship at its simplest: you are both the one who looks and the one being looked at.

The frightening night is worth noticing too. Strong experiences can come early, and Guruji stopped and took time when one scared him. Going slowly and having a teacher to talk to are part of the path.

Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015. Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

Where is the Balaji temple on Naubath Pahad?

The extract places it on Naubath Pahad in Hyderabad, a hill with a stairway built by the Birlas. The book translates the name as Turning-Point Hill. A Hanuman temple stands halfway up.

What happened to Guruji at the Balaji temple?

He prostrated without planning to and felt a thrill through his whole body for ten to fifteen minutes. He saw the universe spin into him and heard Balaji name himself as Venkateswara, Bala and Bala Tripurasundari.

Who was Guruji's first guru?

According to the book, Guruji said that after the 1977 vision Bala became his first spiritual guru. He counted the experience as diksha, a formal initiation.

Is Balaji a form of the Goddess?

In the extract Guruji describes Balaji as a female goddess in a male form, female on the inside and male on the outside. He later saw Balaji mostly as the child goddess Bala Tripurasundari.

What is the book The Goddess and the Guru?

It is a book by Michael Bowden about Guruji, Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati. This post on Guruji's blog is an extract from it.

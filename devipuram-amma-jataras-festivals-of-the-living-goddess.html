Devipuram Amma Jataras: Festivals of the Living Goddess

Guruji explains the five yearly festivals called Jataras, and why he says fun and spirituality go together.

From the teachings of Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji). Post title: "DEVIPURAM AMMA JATARAS: Festivals of the Living Goddess and the Body of Consciousness". Dated 5 November 2016. Posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Guruji says Devipuram means the body of Consciousness. It is the body of every living being, and also a temple place near Anakapalle.
  • He describes Amma, the Mother of all, as a virgin Baala, a mother, a teacher and a lover of Shiva.
  • There are five Jataras (festivals) every year, from Baala Jatara near Guru Pournami to Amma in Sri Chakra in Kartikam and Pushya.
  • Each festival ends with songs, skits with social themes and dance, then arati and prasadam.
  • Guruji says the idea of Amma puja is to recognise that fun and spirituality go together.

Guruji's words

Guruji:

Devi-puram means the body of Consciousness. Devipuram is the body of every living being, including you, me and all. It is also a place between Anakapalle and Sabbavaram where a great temple is made for Devi to live with Her family.

Amma is the mother of all.

Before she is an adult, she is a virgin Baala.
After marriage, She cleanses Herself and makes Ganesha to remove obstacles for creation.
She assembles your body in Her womb combining Her red blood ovum and white seed of Her spouse.
These seeds are made of earth, water, fire, air, space.

She is called Gouri.
She nourishes and protects you against harmful beings and diseases with Her milk, a white form of blood.
She is called Shyama.
She educates you and makes you an adult.
Then she is called Kali or Koli.

Finally She is always sporting with Her lover Shiva.

We celebrate Her gift of life with gratitude in Her devotion, loving Her through a MAD ecstasy of Music, Art and Dance festivals.

Meaning of Jatara

Jatara means a festival.

Five Jataras Every Year

Baala Jatara
Before and after Guru Pournami, because our Guru is Balaji, a virgin form of Amma.

Ganesh Jatara
Around Ganapati Navaratris to remove self imposed obstacles to creativity and procreation.

Durga Puja Time
During Sharada Navaratris – 10 days.

Shyama and Koli
Remaining Aswija masam
(Koli is short for Kola mukhi or Varahi)

Amma in Sri Chakra
Kartikam, Pushya masam.

Common Steps for All Festivals

Draw the yantra of Baala, Ganesha, Durga, Shyama, Koli, and Amma.

Decorate yantras with short (1 meter) poles and decorate seats for people.

Seat people – virgins, singles, couples – to energy centers in yantras.

Invoke 100 jeevakalas to all seated Gods / goddesses. Give panchamrit. TK1

Invoke Amma’s life into each participant. Do puja to all limbs, 16u puja. TK2

Do puja with flowers and gandha pushpa akshatas with LS, Khadgamala. TK3

Cultural Program

Then organize songs, skits with social themes, and dance by all.
The function concludes with arati and naivedya prasadams.

Meaning of Amma Puja

The idea of Amma puja is to recognize that fun and spirituality go together.

Devi is life itself; she is best appreciated by loving life, and creating new life forms of people organizing and working together as one life, with a purpose of enhancing quality of life for every one in the organization.

Three Parts of This Activity

Lose individual identity in group identity.

Develop group consciousness, a sense of belonging to the group.
Every one is called Amma; together they become Baala, Ganapati, Shyama, Koli, or Amma.

Each yantra is self organizing; only information through web feeding it.

It all starts from Devipuram, a place near Anakapalle, circles the world and comes back to Devipuram.
Devipuram is the new name for a new united world.

Amrita-Ananda
(Nonstop Entertainment)

How this shows up in Kaula practice

Guruji speaks of the Goddess as life itself, so the Jataras are a way to thank her for the gift of life. This fits the Kaula view that the Mother is found in the body, in nature and in the people around us.

The festivals use drawn yantras, seats at energy centres and worship of the deities. Guruji also notes that everything is made of earth, water, fire, air and space. The outer worship and the inner sense of the body as her temple belong together.

The details of these rites are not explained here. Guruji points to Devipuram for the festivals themselves. Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015, and Guru Amma Annapurnamba guides the lineage today. This site is an independent practitioner's space and does not speak on behalf of Devipuram.

Common questions

What does Devipuram mean?

Guruji says Devipuram means the body of Consciousness. It is the body of every living being, and also the place between Anakapalle and Sabbavaram where a great temple was made for Devi.

What is a Jatara?

Guruji says Jatara means a festival.

How many Jataras are there each year?

Guruji lists five: Baala Jatara, Ganesh Jatara, Durga Puja time in Sharada Navaratris, Shyama and Koli, and Amma in Sri Chakra in Kartikam and Pushya.

What happens at the end of each festival?

Guruji says there is a cultural program of songs, skits with social themes and dance by all. It ends with arati and prasadam.

What is the idea behind Amma puja?

Guruji says the idea is to recognise that fun and spirituality go together. He says Devi is life itself and is best appreciated by loving life.

Guru Garu Katha: Guruji's Guru, the Avadhuta

Stories and sayings of Sri Swaprakashananda Theertha Avadhuta, the guru of Devipuram Guruji, told by the people who knew him.

A collection about Sri Swaprakashananda Theertha Avadhuta (Guru Garu), Guruji's guru, with accounts by Uma Amma, Shreeram Balijepalli, Kathy Turiyamba, William Thomas, Lalitha Aunty of Mumbai, Lana L. Reichert and Sri Siddhananda Natha, and remarks by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji). Posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 7 November 2016. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Sri Swaprakashananda Theertha Avadhuta, Guruji's guru, was born in 1915 in Vidurbharthi, Andhra Pradesh, and left his body in 2002 as Guru Poornima began.
  • The collection says he took diksha from many teachers, did the sadhana of the 32 Kalikas in Kashi, and in 1980 gave up everything to take sannyasa in Haridwar.
  • He owned almost nothing. Two ochre cloths, a small table and a pocket radio were about all.
  • He gave mantras to anyone who wanted to do japa, whatever their caste, creed or gender.
  • He called Devipuram Guruji the most excellent of his disciples and gave him his blessing to build the temple.
  • To him, Digambara meant giving up desire. Going without clothes had nothing to do with it.

A summary of the collection

Guru Garu Katha is mostly written by Guru Garu's family, disciples and visitors, so this page gives a summary and one short quote. Please read the full collection on Guruji's blog.

The post opens with his full name and a timeline. He was born in 1915 in Vidurbharthi, a village in Andhra Pradesh, and married Sri Sarva Sodulamma in 1926. In 1935 he took diksha from Sri Poornananda Yogi, a disciple of the Tibetan teacher Maru Maharishi, and entered Sri Vidya through Sri Kesari Kameswara Rao. He received Poorna diksha from Sri Gnanananda Saraswathi of Anakapalli in 1947. The compiler writes that he spent three years in Kashi doing the sadhana of the 32 Kalikas, guided by Sri Dakshinamurthi Paramahamsa of Vizag. In 1980 he met Sri Kalyananda Theertha Maharaj in Haridwar, offered him everything he had practised and took sannyasa, later receiving paramahamsa and Digambara diksha. He left his body in 2002, at 6 pm as Guru Poornima began.

Disciples remember a man who kept away from crowds, praise and questions. Uma Amma says he would dodge questions or reply "why do you want to know?", and that "He was blazing like a fire". At Ganganapur he gave his shoulder cloth to an old woman shivering with cold and never wore one again. Gifts did not stay with him. Puja items, watches and perfumes were given away, often in front of the person who brought them.

The longest piece is a memoir by Shreeram Balijepalli, whose father was among Guru Garu's first disciples. He knew Guru Garu as a child, played with his matted locks, and was given Datta mantras while still in school. He describes the volcanic anger that passed in minutes, the rough Telugu humour, trips to Shirdi where Guru Garu stayed outside in the car, and his few possessions: two ochre cloths, a small wooden table, spectacles he never wore and a pocket radio for film songs.

Kathy Turiyamba (1999) and William Thomas (1998) describe visiting him with Guruji and Amma, who told them never to ask him for anything. William Thomas filmed him doing asanas and puja. Lalitha Aunty of Mumbai recalls that he gave her atma sannyasa with a tumbler of water. Lana L. Reichert tells the story of Guru Garu offering Guruji the power to read minds. Guruji did not want it, and later gave it to a cow in the fields at Devipuram.

The post also gathers what Guruji said about his guru, for example "He is aghori and he is formless. He is everything." Guru Garu, for his part, called Guruji "the most excellent of my disciples", and told him that a little girl came to him in meditation and asked him to let Guruji build the temple.

The last part gathers Guru Garu's own sayings, many shared by his direct disciple Sri Siddhananda Natha. He tells seekers to do japa day and night, to keep the mind on the paramatma while living in the world like water on a lotus leaf, and that family life is the best ashrama. He recommends the Avadhuta Gita, Jnana Vasishtha, Bhagavad Gita, Brahma Sutras, the Upanishads and the books of the Ramakrishna Math.

"Just because a beggar is naked or less clothes, he doesn't become a digambara, it is renunciation of desire that makes one a digambara"

Sri Swaprakashananda Theertha Avadhuta, as quoted in "Guru Garu Katha" on Guruji's blog, posted by Vira Chandra on 7 November 2016. The original post includes many photos, which are not copied here.

What this means in practice

Guru Garu is Guruji's guru, the parameshti guru for those who learned from Guruji. Guruji said he invoked all his gurus through the Paduka mantra, so these stories put a face to one of the names behind it.

His advice for householders is plain. Do japa day and night, even while you work. Keep your mind on the Self while you live with your family. "One must immerse in ones own soul instead of dipping in Ganga." In Kaula terms, this is inner worship first, with outer ritual as a support.

He also explained that in samayachara the outer offerings are replaced by symbols, for example self-control in place of madhu, and the Guru's mantras as mantra. We keep to that inner, symbolic reading on this site.

Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015. Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

Who was Devipuram Guruji's guru?

Sri Swaprakashananda Theertha Avadhuta, called Guru Garu or Avadhuta Gurugaru. He lived in Anakapalli and in his last days at Gopalapatnam near Vizag. He left his body in 2002.

When did Guru Garu leave his body?

In 2002, at 6 pm as Guru Poornima began, according to the post. One of his daughters recalled that he told the family three days before and left sitting in padmasana with his eyes open.

What does Digambara Avadhuta mean?

In Guru Garu's words, Digambara means having no desire, not seeking happiness or fame, not giving lectures and not accepting puja. Wearing little or no clothing does not make someone a Digambara.

What did Guru Garu say about Devipuram Guruji?

He called him the most excellent of his disciples and said he had darshan of Devi. He also told Guruji that a little girl came to him in meditation and asked him to let Guruji build the temple, and he promised Guruji the energy of his own sadhana.

Did Guru Garu give mantras to everyone?

Yes. The post says he gave mantras without looking at caste, creed or gender. When people criticised this, he said that those who are interested in doing japa are eligible to take mantras.

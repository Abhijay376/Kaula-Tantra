Guruji's Dreams for the World

Guruji's own words on Nature as a loving Goddess, and the simple, practical dreams he held for every person on Earth.

Written by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji), posted by Vira Chandra. Originally published as "What are my dreams?" at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • Guruji saw Nature as a living, loving Goddess who has guided him through decades of meditation.
  • He built temples, Kamakhya and Sri Meru, as ways of honoring Her creative and nurturing sides.
  • His dreams were practical: clean drinking water, comfortable eco-friendly homes, and light for poor children to study by.
  • He dreamed of protecting the Earth's greenery and the oxygen it gives us.
  • He hoped for a generous world where people with more freely help people with less.
  • He wanted the internet and mobile phones used to carry real spiritual teaching to anyone, anywhere.

In Guruji's own words

"There are two kinds of laws: those made by us and those made by Nature. As long as our laws match those of Nature, She will give us wisdom, bounty, and creative purpose to our lives. When abused and raped, She turns violent. I believe in the power of Nature as a loving Goddess."

"I dream of the days when everyone can get pure water to drink... I dream of the poor getting a light to study after the Sun has set... I dream of a generous world where the haves support the have-nots."

"Love can and should transcend boundaries of country, color, and creed."

These lines are quoted directly from Guruji's post. Read the full piece at the source: "What are my dreams?", posted by Vira Chandra at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com, 2016. Signed by Guruji as Amritananda, Devipuram.

How this shows up in Kaula practice

Kaula practice sees Nature and the five elements as the Goddess Herself, not as separate from Her. Guruji's dream of clean water and protected forests is not just an environmental wish. It is the same reverence for Nature that shows up in ritual worship, now pointed at everyday, practical care for the Earth.

His dream of homes, light, and dignity for the poor points at another side of the same practice: seeing the Goddess in other people, not only in ritual objects. Generosity toward those with less is a direct, simple way to live that recognition, without needing any special occasion.

His comfort with using modern technology for teaching also fits the path. Kaula practice has never insisted on one fixed form. Guruji's own dream was to carry real teaching further, using whatever tools serve that purpose honestly.

Common questions

What were Guruji's dreams for the world?

Guruji dreamed of clean water for everyone, comfortable solar-powered homes, light for poor children to study by, protecting the Earth's greenery, and a world where wealthy people freely help those with less.

Why did Guruji call Nature a loving Goddess?

He said Nature rewards us with wisdom and creativity when we live in line with Her laws, and turns harsh when abused. He described Her as his mentor through decades of meditation, not just an idea.

How did Guruji want technology used?

He wanted the internet and mobile phones used to teach and share spiritual practice with anyone, anywhere, so that learning is not limited by geography or money.

What did Guruji mean by love coming from wisdom?

He believed greed comes from a narrow view of the world, and that real wisdom naturally leads to more generosity and care for others, not less.

How does this connect to Kaula practice?

Kaula practice honors Nature and the five elements as the Goddess in visible form. Guruji's dreams of clean water, protected forests, and shared resources are that same honoring, carried into daily life and service.

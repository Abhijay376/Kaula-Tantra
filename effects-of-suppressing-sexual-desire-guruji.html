Suppressing Sexual Desire: Guruji on the Effects

A short question and answer. Guruji explains what happens when sexual desire is pushed out of sight or held down, and why he saw it as a way into meditation.

Answers by Sri Amritananda Natha Saraswati (Guruji). Post title: "Effects of Repressing and Suppressing Sexual Drive", posted on Guruji's blog by Vira Chandra on 5 November 2016. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • A student asks Guruji about the effects of suppressing sexual desire. He treats repressing and suppressing as two different things.
  • Repressed desire goes underground. He says it pushes a person toward self-defeating acts such as anger, violence and control of others.
  • Suppressed desire shows up as steady inner conflict and anxiety. The ego hardens around it, and that hard shell cuts a person off from energy.
  • Other traditions describe love as merging and widening awareness through full attention. Guruji says sexual activity can be that same meditation, with the sex drive as its material.
  • He ends by calling desire-as-meditation essential for freeing a person from repression.

Guruji's words

Q—What are the various effects of repressing or suppressing the sexual drive?

Guruji: Repressing—comes out as unconscious motivation taking the person towards self-defeating activities—destruction (anger), violence, domination, control of the other/environment.

Suppressing—comes out as constant internal conflict and anxiety, that then has to be shut into the hard shell of the ego (otherwise the anxiety would not allow day-to-day functioning, dealing with the world). Ego, in turn, generates its own effects, such as making the person cut off from any source of energy (stops one from merging).

Love, as understood by many other traditions that apparently ask one to “set aside” the sexual desire (and are thereby held with a certain esteem in society), is to do with merger, with connectivity, with expanding one’s consciousness, making it vast, letting it go into all objects and things. Often this is to be brought about by the act of complete attention.

Sexual activity is a way of doing the same meditations, only here the substance is the sexual drive. (An interesting aspect is to do with why only sex, and not envy or greed, be the substance… (sex is double-edged).) Furthermore, becoming vulnerable, dissolving the ego shell, opening up to energy, unclogging perceptual filters are all things that define any process or method of meditating/Being/Loving.

To use the sexual drive as a way of meditating is not only an excellent or beneficial idea, but also essential to free the individual of any repressions related to this inescapable fact of us/all life. (Kāma—neglected…)

What this means in practice

Guruji's answer is short. In Kaula practice it shows up as a gentle way of meeting desire. You notice the feeling, give it full attention and let that attention widen, which is close to what he calls meditation.

In inner worship you can start small. Sit with a strong feeling, see where it sits in the body, and stay with it without acting on it and without shame. Over time the hard ego shell he describes starts to soften. Our page on suppressed desires has more of his thoughts on the same theme.

Kaula rites that work directly with sexual energy are taught by a living teacher, are explained here only at the level of meaning, and happen only between willing, consenting adults.

Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015. Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

What is the difference between repression and suppression?

In Guruji's answer, repressed desire sinks into the unconscious and drives behaviour without your knowing. Suppressed desire stays in view and is held down, which causes ongoing conflict and anxiety.

What happens if you suppress sexual desire?

Guruji says suppression brings constant inner conflict and anxiety. To get through the day, a person shuts it inside a hard ego shell, and that shell cuts them off from energy.

Can repressed sexual energy cause anger?

In his view, yes. He says repressed desire comes out as hidden motives toward destruction, anger, violence and controlling others.

Can sexual energy be used for meditation?

Guruji says sexual activity can be a way of doing the same meditations other traditions teach, with the sex drive as the material. He calls this essential for freeing a person from repression.

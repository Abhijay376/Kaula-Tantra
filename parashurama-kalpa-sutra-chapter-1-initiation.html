Parashurama Kalpa Sutra, Chapter 1: Diksha

The opening sutras of the classic Sri Vidya manual, on initiation and the doctrine behind it, kept on Guruji's blog.

The Sanskrit sutras of the Parashurama Kalpa Sutra (1.1 to 1.32) with English translation and purports of Yogishanandanatha, as posted on Guruji's blog. The purports are not Guruji's writing. Originally published at amritananda-natha-saraswati.blogspot.com.

In short

  • The Parashurama Kalpa Sutra is a classic Sri Vidya manual, and its first chapter opens with diksha, initiation.
  • Shiva, asked by Bhairavi, gives five traditions through his five faces. The universe is made of 36 tattvas.
  • Shiva wrapped in a body is the jiva. Without the wrapping he is the supreme Shiva, and self inquiry is how you find that out.
  • Mantra works through lineage and faith, and the sutras ask for one guru, no criticism of other paths, and work done without grasping at results.
  • Life itself becomes a fire offering: what you perceive is the oblation, the senses are the ladles, and your own Self is the fire.

A summary of the sutras

This post gives the first 32 sutras of the Parashurama Kalpa Sutra in Sanskrit, each with an English translation and a purport by Yogishanandanatha. The purports are another writer's work, so this page gives a summary and one short quote. The full text is on Guruji's blog.

The chapter begins, "Now we shall expound the initiation." The purport calls diksha the first stop on the long road of sadhana, the staircase to the terrace of liberation, where the teacher plants the seed of realisation in the disciple. Sutra 1.2 tells how Lord Shiva, after setting up the eighteen sciences and the schools of philosophy, was asked by Bhairavi, who is his own consciousness, for the higher knowledge. Through his five faces he gave the five traditions (amnayas), one for each quarter and one for the upper region. The purport lists the eighteen sciences and calls them the lower knowledge, apara vidya, next to the higher knowledge, para vidya.

The doctrine follows. The universe is the 36 tattvas, from the Shiva tattva down to the five elements (1.4). Shiva cloaked in the body is the jiva, and uncloaked he is the supreme Shiva (1.5). The purport names the three bodies, gross, subtle and causal, as the cloak. Turning inward to inquire into yourself is the purpose of life (1.6). Words made of letters are eternal and the power of mantra is beyond thought (1.7, 1.8). Every success comes through tradition and faith (1.9, 1.10), and knowledge of the inner Self dawns when guru, mantra, deity, Self, mind and breath are made one (1.11).

Sutra 1.12 says bliss is the form of Brahman, set within the body, and that the five Ms bring it out, to be used in secret worship. The purport says these are understood differently at different levels of the seeker, and for the purified their meaning is symbolic or substituted. It calls this worship a private communion. Sutra 1.24 adds that if the materials are not available, the worship is done in the mind and that is enough.

Sutras 1.13 to 1.25 give the conduct of the seeker: a firm attitude, no criticism of any system of philosophy, not being moved by what others say of your path, speaking of secret matters only to a fit disciple, steady practice, constant absorption in Shiva, freedom from desire, anger, greed, delusion, pride and jealousy, one guru, no sense of ownership (the purport quotes the Isha Upanishad, "isa vasyam idam sarvam"), action without wanting its fruit, keeping up daily duties, and fearlessness everywhere.

Sutra 1.26 turns all of life into a fire offering. Everything perceived is the oblation, the senses are the ladles, the powers are the flames, your own Self, which is Shiva, is the fire, and you are the one who offers. The fruit is awareness of consciousness free of objects, and nothing is higher than gaining the Self (1.27, 1.28). The chapter closes by saying the Vedas are public while this knowledge is hidden in every school, so the wise should take initiation. There are three kinds: shakti diksha by passing power, shambhavi by the touch of the guru's foot, and mantri by giving the mantra (1.30 to 1.32).

"Shiva cloaked in the body is Jiva; uncloaked he is the supreme Shiva."

What this means in practice

These sutras sit behind much of what a Sri Vidya Kaula student does. Diksha comes first, and the oneness of guru, mantra, deity and Self in sutra 1.11 is what makes a mantra more than a sound. That is why the tradition puts weight on receiving it from a living line.

Sutra 1.26 is a good way to think about inner worship. The homam fire outside has a twin inside: what you see, hear and taste is offered into your own awareness, which the sutra calls Shiva. Daily life becomes the puja.

The five Ms come up in 1.12 and 1.24. The purport reads them at different levels and stresses privacy, and 1.24 says mental worship is enough when materials are not there. Here they are treated only as philosophy. Practice is learned from a guru, never from a page.

The sutras on conduct are plain: one guru, no running down other paths, do your duties, offer the results, be fearless. Guruji attained mahasamadhi in October 2015, and Guru Amma Annapurnamba teaches today.

Common questions

What is the Parashurama Kalpa Sutra?

A classic Sanskrit manual of Sri Vidya worship. Its first chapter, given in this post, deals with initiation and the doctrine behind the practice.

What are the 36 tattvas?

The principles that make up the universe according to sutra 1.4, from the Shiva and Shakti tattvas down through Maya, the mind and senses, to the five elements.

What are the three types of diksha in the Kalpa Sutra?

Sutra 1.32 names shakti diksha, given by passing spiritual power, shambhavi diksha, given by the touch of the guru's foot, and mantri diksha, given by passing the mantra. It says the guru should use all of them.

What does the Kalpa Sutra say about the five Ms?

Sutra 1.12 says they bring out the bliss of Brahman within the body and should be used in private. The purport says their meaning is symbolic for purified seekers, and sutra 1.24 allows mental worship when they are not available.

Why does the Kalpa Sutra say to have one guru?

Sutra 1.20 asks for one guru so there is no room for doubt. The purport explains that several teachers at once give different emphases and confuse the disciple.
